# A Poem About the World of Astronomy

Above the hush of sleeping towns,
A vault of dark, a vault of crowns —
Each pinprick light a patient sun
Whose night-long letter has just begun.

The Moon, that pale and borrowed lamp,
Drags tides across the ocean's ramp;
Mercury runs the inner ring,
And Venus, veiled, refuses spring.

Red Mars remembers ancient seas,
Jupiter wears its storms with ease,
Saturn balances rings of stone,
Uranus tipped, and ice-blue Neptune lone.

Beyond the Kuiper's frozen shelves,
The comets weave among themselves,
And Voyager, our farthest hand,
Still whispers home from soundless land.

The galaxies, like spinning coins,
Are drawn together where space joins;
Andromeda, our nearing kin,
Will fold us where her arms begin.

Black holes keep the time of light,
Bending what they cannot bite;
Quasars shout from younger skies —
A roar from when the cosmos rise.

We are the dust that learned to look,
The page that reads the open book,
A stitch of carbon, brief and bright,
Translating distance into sight.

So when the night is wide and clear,
Step out, breathe slow, and lend an ear:
The universe, in steady tone,
Reminds us we are not alone —
Just small, and lit, and far from done,
A cosmos waking, one by one.
